01

What BlockLock Does

BlockLock is an app-blocking tool. You choose a mode — block a set of apps, allow only a set of apps, or lock the phone down to phone calls only — and a duration. For that duration, BlockLock keeps you off the apps you told it to keep you off. That is the entire product. It does not need to know who you are in order to do it.

02

Data We Collect

BlockLock collects nothing on its own. It does not operate any server, and the app contains no analytics, advertising, or tracking software development kit (SDK) of any kind.

Everything the app needs in order to work is written to a private storage area on your own device and is never transmitted by the app. We never see your contacts, your messages, your browsing activity, your location, or the contents of any app you use. The app makes no network request except those described in Section 09.

The one exception is a problem report. If you use Report a problem, you send us an email from your own email app, with technical details about your phone and BlockLock attached. Nothing is sent unless you press Send, and you can read everything first. Section 10 lists exactly what a report contains.

BlockLock also sets allowBackup="false", so its data is excluded from Android's automatic cloud backup. It stays on the device it was created on.

You can save a copy yourself. Settings → Export settings writes your app lists, schedules, settings and focus history to a file in your phone's Downloads folder, and Progress → Export as CSV shares your daily focus totals. These files are made on your phone, only when you ask, and go only where you put or send them; BlockLock never uploads them. Other apps that are allowed to read your Downloads folder, such as a file manager, can read a file saved there. Settings → Import settings reads such a file back in; it changes only your lists, schedules, settings and focus history, never your purchase or trial.

03

Seeing Which App Is Open

To block an app, BlockLock has to know which app you just opened. It does this with Android's Usage Access permission, which you grant manually in system settings.

BlockLock uses it for one thing only: reading the package name of the app that most recently came to the foreground — the identifier, such as com.example.someapp — so it can check that name against your chosen mode. If the app is not permitted during your session, BlockLock covers it. If it is permitted, nothing happens.

What it does not do: BlockLock never reads the contents of your screen, your keystrokes, or anything inside another app. It does not build a profile of how long you spend in each app, and it does not read your usage history. It asks a single question, twice a second while a session is running: which app is in front right now?

While a session runs, BlockLock also notes in its own activity log on your phone when the app in front changes and what it did about it (for example, “covered”). This log holds package names and times only, stays on your phone, deletes itself after about two days, and leaves the phone only if you send it in a problem report (Section 10).

On some phones, BlockLock also reads, from the same Usage Access, when the screen turned on or off and when the lock screen appeared or went away. These are system events, not app usage; they let BlockLock check that it can show on your lock screen (Section 04).

BlockLock deliberately does not use an Accessibility Service. Accessibility is the more common way for blocking apps to do this, but it is a far broader permission than the job requires, and Android grants it far more reach than reading a package name.

04

Display Over Other Apps

When you open an app that your session does not permit, BlockLock covers it with a plain full-screen panel showing how much time is left. That panel is what the Display over other apps permission is for.

The permission lets BlockLock draw its own window on top of other apps. It does not let BlockLock see, read, or interact with whatever is underneath. Nothing is captured and nothing is recorded — the app draws a cover, and that is all.

Phones that pause apps when the screen goes off. Some phones pause apps a moment after the screen goes off. To keep blocking reliably, BlockLock keeps a tiny invisible window up during a session, and shows its own session screen on top of the lock screen. For that, it asks you to allow BlockLock in the phone's own setting for showing on the lock screen, and to confirm it with a quick check (press the power button twice). That screen shows only the time and how long the session has left; it reads nothing from the lock screen or from your notifications.

05

Device Admin

BlockLock uses Android's Device Administrator permission for two things. Total Lockdown locks your screen for the length of the session. And in every mode, while a session is running, an app that holds this permission cannot be uninstalled, so a session cannot be ended by uninstalling BlockLock. BlockLock asks for it before your first session. This permission sounds alarming, and it is worth being exact about its scope.

BlockLock requests exactly one device-admin policy: force-lock. That is the ability to lock the screen, and nothing else. It cannot erase your phone, change or reset your password, disable your camera, restrict your accounts, or monitor your activity. Those are separate policies, and BlockLock does not request any of them. You can verify this in Android's own device admin settings screen, which lists precisely what an app has asked for.

You can revoke device admin in your phone's settings whenever no session is running (during a session, your phone's Settings app is closed until the session ends). BlockLock never offers to revoke it from inside the app. Once it is revoked, you can uninstall BlockLock normally, and no new session can start until you grant it again.

06

Contacts & Phone Calls

Total Lockdown keeps your phone locked, which would normally also mean you could not make a call. So BlockLock includes its own small dialer and contact list, shown over the lock screen, letting you reach someone without unlocking the device and defeating the session you asked for.

This is optional. Both permissions below are requested only if you turn on “Allow calls during lockdown”. If you leave it off, BlockLock never asks for either, and the dialer is never shown.

READ_CONTACTS
Shows a searchable list of your contacts in BlockLock's own dialer. Your contacts are read from the device each time you open that screen, held in memory only while it is open, and never stored by the app, never written to its files, and never transmitted anywhere. Closing the screen discards them.
CALL_PHONE
Places the call you tap directly, so the phone does not have to be unlocked first. BlockLock does not read your call log, does not know who you have called before, and does not record calls.
07

Other Permissions

The remaining permissions are routine and involve no personal information at all.

FOREGROUND_SERVICE
FOREGROUND_SERVICE_SPECIAL_USE
Keeps blocking running for the whole session. This is also why you see an ongoing notification while a session is active — Android requires it, and it is your way back into the app.
SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM
Ends your session at the exact time it is meant to, and starts scheduled sessions on time. It simply wakes the app at the right moment.
RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED
Lets an active session or a saved schedule resume correctly if your phone restarts, instead of silently losing track of where it was.
REQUEST_IGNORE_
BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS
Asks Android not to shut BlockLock down mid-session to save power. Many phones will otherwise stop it and quietly end your session early. This is a request you can decline.
POST_NOTIFICATIONS
Shows the session countdown, and warns you when something has gone wrong — a permission withdrawn mid-session, or a scheduled session that could not start.
INTERNET
Used solely to talk to Google Play: its billing system (to check what you have bought, to show you the current price, and when you make a purchase) and its update check (to tell you when a newer version of BlockLock is available). BlockLock makes no other network request — see Section 09. A problem report is sent by your own email app, not by BlockLock.
<queries>
To let you pick which apps to block, BlockLock needs the list of apps you can launch. It requests a narrowly scoped view of that list rather than the broad QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES permission, and the list never leaves your device. It also names about twenty of phone makers' own system apps (such as a phone manager or a dual-app tool), so that a problem report can say whether they are installed; your own apps are never listed in a report.
08

What Is Stored On Your Device

For completeness, this is everything BlockLock writes to its private storage. All of it is local, none of it is transmitted, and all of it is deleted when you uninstall the app:

  • Your selected mode, and the app lists you chose for Allowlist and Blocklist
  • The session length you picked last, so it is ready next time
  • Your schedule — days, start time, duration, and mode
  • The current session's end time, and whether calls are allowed during lockdown
  • When your free trial started, how long it is, and whether you have purchased the app
  • The price Google Play last showed for the purchase, so it can be shown to you before you buy
  • How much of today's free Total Lockdown allowance you have used
  • Your focus time for each day (kept for 400 days), your all-time total and the level it has reached, and the times you chose for reminders
  • A count of how many times you opened a blocked app, by app, so the app can show you your own activity. This is kept for 90 days and then automatically deleted, and it records only that an app was opened and blocked — never what you did in it.
  • BlockLock's own activity log: when sessions start and end, when the screen turns on or off, which app came to the front during a session, and what BlockLock did. It holds package names and times only, never more than 1 MB, and deletes itself after about two days. It is used only to put into a problem report you choose to send.
  • On those phones: whether the lock-screen check has passed, and when BlockLock's screen last showed on the lock screen
  • Screenshots you add to a problem report are copied, made smaller and stripped of location data, then deleted within a day

Purchases, Billing & Refunds

BlockLock offers one optional, one-time purchase that unlocks every mode permanently after the free trial. That purchase is processed entirely by Google Play's billing system.

BlockLock never receives, stores, or has any access to your card details, billing address, or any other payment information. It receives only the price Google Play shows for the purchase in your country, and a confirmation from Google Play that the purchase succeeded, which is what unlocks the app. Google's own privacy policy governs that transaction.

Refunds. BlockLock has a free 7-day trial with every feature, so you can decide before you pay. For that reason a purchase is final once it is made. There is nothing to cancel, because there is no subscription.

Try it on your own phone first. Please use the trial to run the modes you plan to use, on the phone you will use them on. Phone makers and Android updates can change how background apps are treated, so BlockLock can behave differently on a new phone or after a major Android update. Your purchase stays on your Google account and can be restored on any phone. If BlockLock stops working properly after a change like this, contact us and we will try to fix it.

Google Play's own refund options, such as its 48-hour window, may still apply, and Google decides those. If something went wrong with your purchase — you were charged twice, or you paid and the app did not unlock — email us with your Google Play order number and we will fix it or refund you. Nothing here limits the rights you have under the law where you live.

If a purchase is refunded, the unlock is removed from the app. That can take a few days to show.

10

Problem Reports

Phones differ a lot in how they treat apps in the background, and a report is how we find out what yours did. Report a problem (the “?” on the home screen, or Settings) is entirely optional. It opens your own email app with:

  • The words you wrote, and any screenshots you chose to add
  • One attached file of technical details: your phone's maker, model and Android version; BlockLock's version and settings (the selected mode, whether a session is running and how long it has left); which permissions BlockLock has; whether any of about twenty phone makers' system apps are installed (a phone manager, a dual-app or second-space tool); and BlockLock's activity log of about the last two days (Section 08)
What a report never contains: your messages, contacts, photos (other than screenshots you add), files, location, or anything from inside any app. Your own installed apps are never listed; the log shows only the package names of apps that were in front during a session.

You can read the attached details on the report screen before sending, and nothing leaves your phone until you press Send in your email app. The email reaches us like any other email, along with your email address, which we use only to reply. We use a report only to find and fix the problem it describes. We do not share it, sell it, or use it for anything else. We keep it until the problem is solved and delete it within 12 months at the latest; email us at any time to have it deleted sooner.

11

Retention & Deletion

Because everything the app keeps lives only in private storage on your device, deleting it is as simple as it should be: uninstalling BlockLock deletes all of it immediately. There is no account to close.

The only copy that can exist anywhere else is a problem report you emailed us (Section 10). Email us to have it deleted; otherwise it is deleted within 12 months.

An export file you saved yourself (Section 02) is yours, not part of the app's storage: it stays in your Downloads folder, or wherever you sent it, until you delete it.

Children's Privacy

BlockLock is not directed at children and is not designed to collect information from anyone, of any age — consistent with the rest of this policy, it collects no personal information on its own; the only information we ever receive is a problem report someone chooses to email us.
